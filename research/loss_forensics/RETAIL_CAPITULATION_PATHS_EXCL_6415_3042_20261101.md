# Retail Capitulation Paths — Cross-sectional 2026
Date: 2026-11-01
Status: retrospective loss forensics, not a trading model

Excluded tickers: 6415, 3042.
Sample: 503 complete peak-to-trough legs from the remaining bound electronics universe.

## Price-path taxonomy
These classes are mutually exclusive descriptive buckets, used only to understand how a decline feels to the holder.

1. Rebound-torture path: 169 legs (33.6%). At least one rebound of 5% or more from a running low before the final trough, unless already classified as fast-crash or slow-grind. Median total decline about -22.8%.
2. One-way deceleration path: 107 legs (21.3%). No meaningful 5% rebound and high directional path efficiency. Median decline about -17.8%.
3. Mixed decline: 84 legs (16.7%). Neither extreme; alternating small recoveries and new lows. Median decline about -16.9%.
4. Fast-crash path: 80 legs (15.9%). Reaches -10% within roughly two trading sessions and the complete leg finishes within about seven sessions. Median decline about -18.6%.
5. Slow-grind path: 63 legs (12.5%). Long-duration erosion; descriptive rule uses at least 18 trading sessions and at least five sessions before the first -10%. Median decline about -22.7%.

The most common pain path is therefore not a single crash. It is repeated hope-reset followed by another low.

## Capital-structure overlays
These are overlapping mechanisms, not mutually exclusive categories.

- Peak leverage positive: 303 / 503 (60.2%).
- Visible institutional flow flips from positive at the peak to negative next session: 277 / 503 (55.1%).
- First post-peak Big400 outflow: 368 / 503 (73.2%).
- Big400 down plus holder count up: 205 / 503 (40.8%).
- Failed ownership repair: 229 / 503 (45.5%). Median complete decline about -21.9%.
- Ownership repair after first outflow: 139 / 503 (27.6%). Median complete decline about -18.2%.
- Strict euphoria-handoff combination in the existing audit definition: 84 / 503 (16.7%).

Failed repair is especially common in slow-grind and rebound-torture paths, suggesting that prolonged psychological pressure often coincides with persistent ownership deterioration rather than a single one-day exit.

## Current interpretation
There is no single universal mechanism. A holder can be forced into a late sale through several distinct paths:

- sudden loss of price support and fast drawdown;
- long-duration erosion that exhausts conviction;
- repeated rebounds that restore hope and then fail;
- persistent ownership leakage without strong-holder repair;
- faster professional flow reversing while leveraged positions remain;
- relative abandonment when capital relocates to another stock and the held stock loses sponsorship.

These mechanisms can overlap in one episode. The research object should therefore be a state transition graph rather than one pattern or one stock template.
